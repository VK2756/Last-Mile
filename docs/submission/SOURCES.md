# Sources for real-world claims

Use these primary sources for the pitch and submission. Paraphrase; the product's outcome numbers must come from its own Monte Carlo evaluator, with the simulation assumptions displayed.

- **Camp Fire, 2018:** NIST's [Camp Fire case study](https://www.nist.gov/publications/case-study-camp-fire-notification-evacuation-traffic-and-temporary-refuge-areas-nettra) reports about 40,000 people evacuated, 85 fatalities, and more than 18,000 destroyed structures. It examines notification, traffic, temporary refuge, rescues, and fatalities. Use only if a historical example is needed.
- **Evacuation timing and route hazards:** NIST's [ESCAPE guidance](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/wildland-urban-interface-wui-fire-data-collection-parcel-vulnerabilities/escape) describes how fast moving fire can overtake warnings and block roads. This supports modeling changing routes and time to impact; it does **not** validate our simulated outcomes.
- **Accessibility needs:** CDC's [Emergency Preparedness and Disability Inclusion](https://www.cdc.gov/disability-emergency-preparedness/about/) explains that evacuation plans and emergency communications can miss disability related needs and that transportation and support disruptions create barriers.
- **Transportation planning:** FEMA's [preparedness flyer for older adults and people with disabilities](https://www.ready.gov/sites/default/files/2023-05/ready.gov_older-adults_flyer.pdf) advises planning transportation, communications, medicines, and assistive devices ahead of an evacuation.
